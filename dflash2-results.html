Root cause of boot 5

vLLM's hybrid KV manager sizes every pool block by the widest group. The image kept the drafter's 5 layers in one group (5 × 6.16 MB) while each target layer had its own (3.27 MB). Every block was 30.8 MB, each target layer used 3.27 MB of it.

Fix: plugin module kvgroups.py

One KV group per drafter layer, and the drafter's block shrunk 3008 → 1504 tokens so its page fits under the target's. vLLM allows per-group block sizes (scheduler on the lcm, prefix hashing on the gcd). Kill switch DF2_DRAFT_BLOCK_SIZE=off. No image patch.

Measure

  • Done, night 2: 4 drafts (FLASH_LOCAL_DRAFT_TOKENS=4, a flag of the existing target; the drafter's config says block_size 8, the card's 3 was SGLang's QSA verify limit). Result: 2.88 of 5 accepted, +8 to 9% aggregate at 4 to 5 streams, +4 to 6% at 6, level at 7 to 8. Next: 5 drafts only after a clean single-stream bench at 4; geometric decay says position 5 accepts ~0.20, +0.2 tokens a step (3.08 of 6) against a step 8 to 10% costlier: about level at the 7 to 8 streams this box runs at night, a few percent at 4 to 6.
  • Controlled single-stream ./bench.sh, armed to run when the box is idle (the first arming fired into the 2026-11-10 restart window and is re-armed with an engine-up check). MTP reference: greedy median 55.1 to 55.4 tok/s. Fills the TBD column in BENCHMARKS.md.
  • Quality battery (GSM8K, HumanEval) on this target, once. Lossless by construction, but the lane's numbers should be reproduced.
  • Lane tuning, not drafter: while a long prompt prefills, every decode stream gets one step per 4096-token chunk, about 1.9 s on this box (aggregate fell to 8 to 12 tok/s at 6 to 7 streams for minutes, on MTP too). A smaller --max-num-batched-tokens (1024 to 2048) would keep streams responsive during prefill at some cost to prefill throughput; worth one measured try.

Decide

  • Verdict after two nights: MTP stays the lane's default. DFlash2 at 4 drafts trails 5 to 14% in aggregate decode at 4 to 6 streams (acceptance 2.88 against 3.1 to 3.4) and wins on KV headroom (plus 30%); at 3 drafts it trailed 7 to 20% and won on step latency too. Keep it as a documented second target, and make 4 the target's draft default (the installer's is 3).
  • Drafting more is a flag, not a retrain (corrected: an earlier version of this slide said the block was 4 by training; the config says 8). The geometric projection for 4 drafts was 2.87; measured 2.88, position 4 at 0.28: the block-8 training gives no bonus on later positions, so 5 drafts projects ~3.08 and no more. The teacher mismatch is the lever for the rest of the gap.

Fine-tuning the DFlash2 drafter for FP4-Local

Same drafter, same plugin, same unit. Only the teacher changes: the FP4-Local target instead of the FP8 checkpoint. Two ways to get there.

What has to change, and what does not

  • Drafter stays BF16, 0.6B, same architecture, same plugin, same KV grouping and boot path. Quantizing it buys nothing and costs acceptance.
  • Teacher becomes the FP4-Local target: hidden states captured from it, training text regenerated by it. The paper's recipe with our checkpoint as teacher.
  • Block stays 8, as incoai trained it. The draft depth is the unit's flag (1 to 7), not a property of the weights; the card's 3 was SGLang's QSA limit.
  • Measured the same way: the metrics collector and bench already in place give acceptance per position and tok/s by concurrency for any drafter we boot.

Why it should work

  • The gap is 0.3 to 0.6 accepted tokens per step, the size of a teacher mismatch, not of a method that fails: on predictable text this drafter already accepts 3.7 to 4.0 of 4 on the FP4 target.
  • The MTP head has no such headroom: it ships inside the checkpoint and is already matched to it.
  • A drafter back at the card's 2.8 to 3.3 at 3 drafts ties our MTP at a cheaper step; 4 or 5 drafts at the card's per-position rates give 3.3 to 3.7.
